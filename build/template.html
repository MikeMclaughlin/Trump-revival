Sources (all retrieved Oct 21, 2026) and caveats

  • Layer 1, 1940s-1980s and the post-WWII peak (1946-1989): BLS State and Area CES, SIC basis (discontinued 2003), statewide manufacturing, all employees, not seasonally adjusted annual averages, series SAU{state}00003000011. download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/sa/. Series start in 1939 except AK (1960), HI (1958), IL (1947), MI (1956), MN (1950). Peaks in those 5 states may be missed and are flagged in the tooltip.
  • Layer 1, 1990-today: BLS State and Area CES, NAICS basis, statewide manufacturing, all employees, NSA, series SMU{state}000003000000001, data through Sep 2026 (Aug-Sep 2026 preliminary). download.bls.gov/pub/time.series/sm/. "Today" = average of the latest 12 months (Oct 2025-Sep 2026), so seasonality cancels out.
  • Peak definition: highest annual average from 1946 on, using SIC for 1946-1989 and NAICS for 1990-2025. SIC "manufacturing" included some activity (e.g. publishing) that NAICS moved out, so pre-1990 levels run a few percent higher than a like-for-like NAICS figure would. The national peak (BLS CES national series CES3000000001) was 19,553,000 in June 1979. Sep 2026: 12,652,000 (preliminary). Jan 2025: 12,673,000.
  • Layer 1, 1900s-1920s: US Census Bureau, Location of Manufactures, 1899-1929 (1933), Table 18, "wage jobs" = average number of wage earners for the year, census years 1899, 1919 and 1929. Used as the representative year for the 1900s, 1910s and 1920s; no 1909 figure is in this table. Wage earners exclude salaried staff and proprietors (about 15% of factory employment in 1929), so these are not directly comparable with later BLS all-employee counts. Values were hand-transcribed from the scanned PDF. Checked against the printed division totals, four division-years differ by 3 to 33 jobs (under 0.01%), which points to small digit-reading uncertainty in one state each. AK and HI are not in the table. AZ, NM and OK were territories in 1899 but are included because the Census table lists them. DC's -95% drop from its 1967 peak is partly definitional: SIC counted newspaper publishing as manufacturing. 1930s = BLS 1939 annual average, the only 1930s year in the BLS series.
  • Layer 2: USAspending.gov API v2 (spending_by_award plus award detail), filtered to new awards with first action on or after Jan 1, 2016, through Oct 21, 2026. Selection: top 600 contracts in NAICS 31-33 (manufacturing, including defense production), top 120 contracts in NAICS 23 and 2211-2213 (construction and utilities), and top 160 grants from the Departments of Energy, Commerce and Transportation, all by award amount (880 records, 879 distinct awards; 1 award appeared in two lists and is shown once). Excluded: 25 awards performed abroad and 6 awards whose period of performance began before Jan 1, 2016 (listed in layer2_awards_excluded.csv). Administration = who was in office on the date the award was signed (Obama to Jan 19, 2017; Trump I Jan 20, 2017 to Jan 19, 2021; Biden Jan 20, 2021 to Jan 19, 2025; Trump II from Jan 20, 2025). Later modifications can add obligations under a later administration; the whole amount is tagged by signing date. Located by place-of-performance ZIP (Census 2024 ZCTA Gazetteer), county centroid, or recipient ZIP when no place was given (flagged in the tooltip). "Amount" is total obligated to date, not money paid out (outlays), and multi-year contracts are obligated in increments.
  • Layer 3: USAspending.gov API v2 spending_by_geography, place of performance, by state, summing obligations by action date in each window. Contracts = award types A-D. Grants = 02-05. "All" also includes direct payments (e.g. Medicare and Social Security-type programs), loans at face value, insurance and IDVs. Territories and unknown locations are excluded from the state map; they make up about 2-4% of the national total. Check: the state sums are 96% (contracts) and 98% (grants) of the national spending_over_time totals for the same window. Grant data for FY2026 lags (FY2026 grants so far are $352B vs. $624B in FY2025's Jan 20-Sep 30 part), so recent months are understated. 2000s = Oct 1, 2007-Dec 31, 2009 only: the search API starts at FY2008, and full FY2001+ data is only in bulk downloads. Decades before the 2000s have no data. Per capita uses Census Vintage 2025 estimates (Trump period), the April 2020 estimates base (2020-2025 window) and the Census 2010 count (2000s and 2010s).
  • Layer 4: today = senate.gov senators_cfm.xml (100 senators: 53 R, 45 D, 2 I; matches senate.gov's party division page). Past decades = Voteview HSall_members.csv, first occupant of each seat in the first Congress convened in that decade (57th/1901, 62nd/1911 ... 112th/2011). Party codes are Voteview's. Third parties (Populist, Progressive, Farmer-Labor etc.) are shown as "other".
  • Layer 5: today = Clerk of the House MemberData.xml (publish date Oct 1, 2026): 218 R, 214 D, 1 I (Kevin Kiley, CA-03, caucuses R), 2 vacancies (FL-20, TX-23). This matches clerk.house.gov's live party breakdown on Oct 21, 2026. Districts: Census 2024 cartographic boundaries for the 119th Congress (cb_2024_us_cd119_20m), which are the districts current members were elected from. Mid-decade redraws that take effect for the 2026 election are not shown. Past decades: Voteview as for the Senate. DC's non-voting delegate is excluded.
  • Layer 6 (Power & fusion investments), Jan 20, 2025 to Oct 21, 2026: USAspending.gov API v2, every award first made on or after Jan 20, 2025, with no top-N cap and no minimum amount. Each pull was paged to the end and its record count checked against USAspending's own count: all Department of Energy grants and cooperative agreements (1,740), other financial assistance (245), definitive contracts and orders (2,870) and loans (10); USDA Rural Electrification loans and loan guarantees (10.850, 137); USDA Rural Energy for America grants and loans plus High Energy Cost grants (10.868, 10.859; 453); and all-agency awards matching "microreactor", "small modular reactor" or "fusion energy". Records were sorted into power categories by program and by power terms in the award title. Not counted: non-power items (critical minerals, carbon storage, nonproliferation, weapons and basic nuclear physics, energy efficiency, buildings, vehicles, gas pipelines) and facility upkeep (janitorial, lawn care, HVAC, roofing, software licenses, audits, legal and admin support). Everything is consolidated: grants, contracts, loans (counted at face value; the subsidy cost is in the tooltip and CSV) and announced awards are added into one "total federal funding" figure per project, per state and per category. Pieces are grouped into one project (one marker) when they share recipient, site and category. Constellation Crane is counted at DOE's $1.0B (USAspending shows $3.0B face value; DOE and Constellation's SEC 8-K say $1.0B). "Conditional commitment" loans (Westinghouse ANDP, $17.5B; SHINE, $263M) are recorded in USAspending but have not closed. Announced, not yet obligated = official DOE selections with no matching USAspending award as of Oct 21, 2026: TVA and Holtec small modular reactors (Dec 2, 2025), four coal-recommissioning Topic 1 selections, and the remainders of the $500M Defense Production Act coal program (Jun 4, 2026) and the $134.1M FIRE + INFUSE fusion program (Sep 10, 2025). Each remainder is the announced amount minus the matching awards already in USAspending. The $175M rural coal announcement (Feb 12, 2026) is now fully obligated to its six named utilities, so nothing extra is counted for it. Announced amounts are included in the totals and flagged with a dashed marker outline. No private investment is included. Point locations come from the place-of-performance ZIP or county, otherwise the recipient ZIP (flagged). AEP Texas is placed at the Texas center (statewide program). Westinghouse ANDP is plotted at the recipient HQ and left out of state totals (reactor sites not named). Not in USAspending, so not shown: TVA and Bonneville spending (self-funded), Milestone-program fusion payments, Interior/Reclamation hydropower, DOE contract vehicles (IDVs) until orders are placed, and Palisades (its DOE loan closed Sep 2024, before this period).
  • Base map: Census 2024 cartographic boundary file cb_2024_us_state_20m (simplified), Albers USA projection with AK and HI inset. Population: Census NST-EST2025-ALLDATA and st-est00int-alldata.
